## âš–ï¸ Legal Charge / Lien on Property

Yes, this is standard in deferred payment deals:

- A **legal charge** is registered at the Land Registry against the investor's property
- It gives you security: if they don't pay, you can force a sale of that property (in theory)
- **But** you need:
  - **Your own solicitor** to draft and register the charge â€” NOT the investor's solicitor
  - **Verification** that the investor's property is unencumbered or has sufficient equity
  - **Clear terms**: interest rate, payment schedule, default triggers, enforcement timeline
  - **Personal guarantee** from the investor (not just the property)

**Critical**: If the investor's property already has a mortgage, your charge sits behind the lender. If they default and you try to enforce, the mortgage lender gets paid first. You need to know the loan-to-value on their property.
