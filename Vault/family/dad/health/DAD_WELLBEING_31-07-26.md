## Health intelligence
- **DVT risk stack**: NHS lists age over 60, limited mobility, and dehydration as independent risk raisers. Dad has all three plus a dilated vessel — this is the highest-priority monitorable.
- Warning signs to act on same-day: throbbing pain or swelling in the remaining leg, skin red/blue/darker than usual, swollen veins. With shortness of breath or chest pain → 999 (pulmonary embolism).
- NHS advice: do not stay still for long periods; change position roughly hourly. Keep the leg raised when seated. Compression stockings only if prescribed — check with the team given his vessel condition.
- **Stump care (NHS)**: wash daily (more in hot weather) with mild unscented soap and warm water, dry carefully. Do not soak — prolonged water softens skin and makes injury likely. Check daily for redness, breaks, or rubbing.
- **Prostate post-prostatectomy**: routine follow-up at 92 is usually PSA monitoring only; confirm the interval with the GP if it's lapsed.

## Wellbeing tips
- Hydration is the cheapest DVT and UTI protection — steady fluids through the day, not one big drink. Watch dark urine.
- Pressure-area relief: repositioning/weight shifts every 1–2 hours in the chair; protein and vitamin C support skin integrity and healing.
- Seated exercises — ankle pumps, calf raises on the intact leg, arm circles — several times daily for circulation.
- Mental wellbeing: appetite loss, withdrawal, and disturbed sleep are the common markers of low mood in elderly amputees. Regular visits/calls and a predictable daily rhythm matter as much as the clinical side. Phantom limb sensation is normal and treatable — worth raising, not enduring.
