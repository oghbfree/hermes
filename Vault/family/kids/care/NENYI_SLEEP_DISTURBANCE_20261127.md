## 🛏️ PRESENTING ISSUE

**Pattern:** Waking up in the middle of the night, staying awake in bed on laptop

**What Nenyi says:** "I can't sleep"

**Contributing factors:**
- 🔴 **Had bad dreams** (at least once — may be recurring)
- 🔴 **Screen time at night** — using laptop in bed when can't sleep
- 🔴 **Anxiety about sleeping** — staying awake to avoid dreams

---

## ⚠️ RED FLAGS

### **1. Screen Time Making It Worse**
- **Blue light suppresses melatonin** — laptop in bed delays sleep onset even more
- **Stimulation keeps brain active** — videos/games/browsing prevents wind-down
- **Creates dependency** — "can't sleep → use laptop" becomes the loop

### **2. Dream Anxiety Pattern**
- Had bad dreams → now afraid to fall asleep
- Staying awake is **avoidance behavior** (ASD kids often use avoidance for fear)
- **Without intervention, this becomes chronic insomnia**

### **3. Speech Therapy Impact**
Nenyi is already in speech therapy (week 16-20 Nov report shows Fair performance, working on slowing down). **Sleep deprivation will:**
- Reduce speech progress (executive function needs rest)
- Increase rushing when speaking (fatigue → less control)
- Lower frustration tolerance at school

---

## 📋 IMMEDIATE ACTIONS (TONIGHT/TOMORROW)

### **STEP 1: Remove Laptop from Bedroom (Non-negotiable)**

**Why:** Screen time at night is **actively preventing sleep**, not helping it.

**How:**
1. **Talk to Nenyi BEFORE bedtime (not in the moment):**
   > "I know you have trouble sleeping sometimes. The laptop light makes it harder for your brain to sleep. Starting tonight, the laptop stays in the living room after 7 PM. If you can't sleep, I have other things you can do instead."

2. **New rule:** Laptop/tablet/phone stays outside bedroom from 7 PM onwards (every night, no exceptions)

3. **Expect resistance the first 2 nights** — this is normal. Hold the boundary.

---

### **STEP 2: Create Safe Alternatives for Night Wakings**

**When Nenyi wakes and can't sleep, he needs LOW-STIMULATION options:**

**Option 1: Audio stories (best for falling back asleep)**
- Audiobook or calm story podcast (no screen)
- Leave a small speaker in room with pre-loaded playlist
- Suggests: "If you wake up, press play. Close your eyes and listen."

**Option 2: Reading light + physical book**
- Dim red reading light (red light doesn't suppress melatonin like blue)
- Boring book (not exciting comics — something repetitive/calming)
- Example: fact books, picture dictionaries, nature books

**Option 3: Breathing/relaxation**
- Teach **4-7-8 breathing** (breathe in 4 counts, hold 7, out 8 — repeat 4 times)
- Or body scan: "Relax your toes, then feet, then legs..." (works up to head)

**What NOT to do:**
- ❌ Let him get up and play
- ❌ Let him use screens
- ❌ Bring him to your bed every time (builds dependency)

---

### **STEP 3: Address the Bad Dreams (Daytime Conversation)**

**Tomorrow or this weekend, talk to Nenyi when he's calm (NOT at bedtime):**

**Questions to ask:**
1. "Tell me about the bad dream you had. What happened?"
2. "Do you have bad dreams a lot, or was it just once?"
3. "What do you think about when you're trying to fall asleep?"

**Listen for:**
- ✅ Specific fears (monsters, being lost, someone hurt, school stress, money incident?)
- ✅ How often (once vs. recurring pattern)
- ✅ What he does when scared (stays awake, comes to you, uses laptop)

**After listening:**
- **Validate:** "Bad dreams are scary. I understand why you don't want to go back to sleep."
- **Reframe:** "Dreams can't hurt you. Your brain makes pictures when you sleep — they're not real."
- **Empower:** "If you have a bad dream, you can wake up and tell it to go away. Then think about something nice instead (family, drawing, favorite toy)."

---

### **STEP 4: Bedtime Routine Reboot (Start Tomorrow Night)**

**Consistent routine = sleep cue for the brain**

**Suggested routine (7:00 PM - 8:00 PM):**
1. **7:00 PM — Devices dock** (laptop/tablet to living room, no negotiation)
2. **7:15 PM — Bath or shower** (warm water relaxes body)
3. **7:30 PM — Calm activity** (drawing, lego, reading with you — NO screens)
4. **7:45 PM — Bedroom wind-down** (read a story together, dim lights)
5. **8:00 PM — Lights out** (audiobook or white noise ON, say goodnight, leave)

**Key rule:** Same time, same order, every night (even weekends). Brains love predictability.

## 🩺 WHEN TO ESCALATE

**See a doctor/therapist if:**
- Night wakings continue >3x/week after 2 weeks of intervention
- Nenyi shows signs of anxiety during the day (clinginess, refusal to go places, school avoidance)
- Dreams are traumatic (recurring nightmares about violence, death, harm)
- Sleep deprivation affects school (too tired to focus, falling asleep in class)
- Bedwetting starts (new regression — can be stress/anxiety)

**Intervention now prevents bigger problems later.**
