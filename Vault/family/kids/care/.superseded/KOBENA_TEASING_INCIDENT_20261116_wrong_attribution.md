# Kobena teased by Nenyi — "big belly kobena" (16/11/26)

> **Reported by H, 16/11/26:** Kobena came home crying and **reciting** "big belly kobena" — the phrase
> **"Ti"** used to say to him to tease him. Mum asked why he was crying; **Nenyi said it was because he
> (Nenyi) had been saying it.** H: "it seems to have distressed him a little."

---

## The most important detail: he was RECITING it, not reporting it

Kobena (11, ASD) came home **repeating the phrase**, not saying "Nenyi called me big belly."

That is **delayed echolalia** — replaying a heard phrase — which is a core feature of how autistic
children process and communicate. Three practical consequences:

1. **The words ARE the report.** He may not have the language to say "I felt humiliated." The phrase
   carries the feeling. Treat the recitation as him telling you what happened.
2. **The phrase has become attached to the hurt.** It is now a script he associates with shame. That
   is why it can keep surfacing for days *after* the teasing has stopped — **repetition is not proof he
   is still being teased**, so don't treat every repetition as a fresh incident.
3. **Do not tell him to stop repeating it.** Suppressing it removes his only channel for the distress.
   The move is to *replace* the script, not delete it (see below).

the whole family can name it as not-allowed.

## What to do — using the family's own framework, not a new one

### With Kobena, first, in the moment
- **Believe him and name it:** *"That hurt you. You're allowed to be upset."* Nothing else in the
  moment. Do not say "it's just teasing" or "he didn't mean it" — that tells him his hurt is wrong.
- **Give him a usable script to replace the one he's reciting:**
  *"You can say: 'Don't say that. It hurts.'"* Practise it once, calmly. He needs an exit line more
  than he needs a lecture.
- **Do not discuss his body.** Not reassurance ("you're not big"), not correction. Both keep the
  frame alive. Reassure his **worth**, not his measurements: *"You're my son and you're enough."*
- **Let the recitation run.** Sit with him through it. It will fade once the phrase stops being said
  and the hurt is acknowledged.

### With Nenyi, per the framework (not mid-crisis)
The framework's own entry for exactly this — "Fighting with Kobena (over toys, **name-calling**)":

1. **Separation.** Different parts of the house, 10–15 min, quiet activity each.
   **No lecture mid-fight.**
2. **Later, restorative:** *"What happened? What could you do differently?"* — not "why did you say
   that?" (an accusation dressed as a question invites a lie).
3. **Repair by action, not apology alone.** He's the drawer — have him **make Kobena something**.
   A drawing is his real currency and it makes the repair concrete rather than mumbled.
4. Point out the consequence he can actually see: *"He came home crying. That's what your words did."*
   Nenyi is verbally typical — he can hold that.

### The rule that must now exist, said out loud, to both
> **"We don't say 'big belly' in this house. Not as a joke, not when you're annoyed, not ever."**

Say it plainly and once. If the phrase was ever a family joke ("Ti used to say it"), **that is the
root** — children repeat what they hear adults laugh at. It cannot be half-banned.

## Warning signs to escalate on
- The phrase intruding at **other times** (mealtimes, bedtime, unprompted) — suggests it has become
  intrusive rather than residual.
- **Food refusal, hiding his body, avoiding changing in front of others, not wanting school.**
- Any **new self-directed comment** about his body.
- Nenyi escalating: repeated targeting of the same vulnerability rather than one-off teasing.
