# DR. UMAR JOHNSON — REWARDS & CONSEQUENCES FRAMEWORK

> **Applied to:** Kobena (11, ASD) & Nenyi (10, typical) — integrated into their daily routines and life progress.
> **Philosophy:** Dr. Umar Johnson's approach — no physical punishment, natural/logical consequences, privilege-based, restorative not punitive. Discipline = teaching the child to be a responsible member of the family/community, not control.

## The Core Principles (Dr. Umar's Model)

### 1. No hitting, no shouting — ever
Discipline is *teaching*, not violence or intimidation. The adult regulates their emotions first before addressing the child's behaviour. Calm delivery every time.

### 2. Behaviour = communication
All behaviour — including Kobena's hand-shaking + 'no', echolalia, self-slapping — is a message about an unmet need, not defiance. The consequence isn't the first response; understanding the need is.

### 3. Privileges are earned, not automatic
Screen time, treats, toys, special outings: these are *privileges* that flow from demonstrated responsibility. Not bribes ("if you do X you get Y") — the child understands that contributing to the household is the path to greater freedom.

### 4. Natural & logical consequences
- **Natural:** What happens naturally if you don't intervene (e.g. don't put toys away → you can't find them next time)
- **Logical:** Consequence directly relates to the infraction (e.g. screen-end battle → lose tomorrow's screen block, not "no dinner")

### 5. Restorative, not punitive
The goal is understanding + repair: "You broke it. What can you do to fix it or make it right?" This builds conscience and empathy, not resentment.

### 6. Consistency is everything
Same rule, same consequence, every time — delivered matter-of-factly. No exceptions depending on mood or convenience.

### 7. Structure creates freedom
Children feel safe with clear boundaries. The routine (see `SUMMER_ROUTINE_2026.md`) IS the first layer of discipline — a predictable day prevents most power struggles before they start.

## Applied to Kobena (ASD, 11)

### How his framework differs
Kobena processes consequences differently. His executive function / receptive language track younger — so consequences must be **immediate, concrete, visual, and paired with the routine he already knows**. A verbal "you lost screen time tomorrow" won't land the same way.

### Kobena's consequence system
| Behaviour / moment | Natural/Logical Consequence | Delivery |
|---|---|---|
| Screen-end meltdown (banging, hitting bed/shaking) | Screen session **ends** — it doesn't extend. Use timer + warning. If meltdown happens, **calmly hold the boundary**: "Finished. Timer's done. [next activity]." Don't negotiate | Calm, low-voice. The timer IS the authority, not your voice |
| Self-slapping / hitting during demand | **Pause the demand.** Step back. Reduce the pressure. Consequence is NOT punishment — it's removing the demand-trigger and returning to regulation first | "I see you're upset. Let's take a break." The consequence is the *adult slowing down*, not the child losing something |
| Refusal cue (hand-shake + "no") | **Respected, not fought.** He's communicating. Offer one alternative, then move on. No power struggle | "Okay, no Lego. Want cars or book instead?" |
| Physical aggression (hitting/slapping others — rare, but noted) | **Immediate quiet removal** from the activity/room. 5-min calm-down. Then a short talk at his level: "Hands are for helping, not hitting." No lecture beyond 2 sentences | Remove, calm, brief phrase. Then reconciliation — hug/return |
| Won't follow routine step | **Visual does the work.** Point to the now/next board or schedule. "Look — next is [X]." The visual IS the authority | Low words, high pointing. Scaffold the step (guide hand, do it with him) |

### Rewards for Kobena
There are no "prizes" in Dr. Umar's model. Instead:
- **Recognition** — catch him doing right, name it simply: "You put the Lego away yourself. Good job."
- **Earned privilege** — consistent morning routine → choice of what to do in a preferred slot
- **Brother connection** — a calm morning means he gets to choose the shared activity later
- **Physical affection** — hugs, high-fives, hair ruffles — he responds to calm proprioceptive connection
- **"Helper" role** — trusted to do a small task (carry something, open the door) builds the sense of contribution

## Applied to Nenyi (younger brother, ~10, typical)

Nenyi is the younger brother but verbally/developmentally typical. He learns from reason + structure — and as the younger sibling, some of his behaviours are about keeping up with/competing with his older brother

### Consequences (by severity)
| Infraction | Consequence | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Screen time limit ignored (not turning off) | **Loses next day's screen block.** Set the timer together. When it rings, device goes to parent until next scheduled slot. No arguing the timer | He may test the boundary once — hold it. After that, consistency ends the testing |
| Fighting with Kobena (over toys, name-calling) | **Separation.** They go to different parts of the house/room. Each does a quiet activity alone for 10–15 min. No lecture mid-fight. Later, a **restorative conversation**: "What happened? What could you do differently?" | Younger but can articulate — this builds his conscience and social understanding |
| Rude/disrespectful tone or backtalk | **Not matched with anger.** Calm "That tone doesn't work in this house. Try again." If repeats, **loss of a privilege** (not screens — something relational, like "that means you stay at the table while we finish dinner" — he sits, no phone, listens to family) | Teach tone is about how you speak to people, not about winning |
| Not completing a responsibility (e.g. putting things away, not drawing when he said he would) | **Natural:** his stuff stays where he left it. If he can't find it later, that's the consequence. Logical: "You said you'd draw for 30 min. If you didn't, drawing time is what we had — it's done." | Use his own words/commitments. Teach responsibility for promises |
| Helping/prosocial behaviour | **Recognised.** "You shared the timer. That was grown." Not a reward — verbal recognition IS the reward in this model | This builds his internal motivation, not "what do I get for it" |

### Nenyi's earned privileges
Dr. Umar's model says privileges come from responsibility:
- **"Timer-keeper"** — trusted to manage both his and Kobena's screen timer → builds: responsibility + authority + a constructive leadership role in the brother relationship
- **"Gallery curator"** — if he draws consistently, his drawings go on the wall → the privilege IS the display
- **Co-watch selector** — responsible handling of his screen time → he can choose what he and Kobe watch together (shared) — this becomes a privilege, not a right
- **Kitchen helper** — trusted to help with cooking → builds contribution + life skills

## The Daily Rhythm (merged into SUMMER_ROUTINE)

**Morning huddle (quick):** "Boys, today: morning outside, then Lego/drawing, then screen. Who's got the timer today?"

**Screen transition:** 5-min warning → timer fires → device to parent. No negotiation. The timer rules, not the parent. Consistency = no testing within ~3 days.

**End-of-day check-in (brief):**
- "What went well today?" (each says one thing)
- "What's one thing we do better tomorrow?"
- *This replaces "punishments" — the child participates in their own growth*

**Consequence delivery:** Calm · Matter-of-fact · Now/next framed. Never in anger, never with a threat.

## Integration with Kobena's specific needs
- His consequences are **immediate + visual** — not stacked or delayed. "No screen tomorrow" doesn't register in the moment. The current screen ending is enough.
- His reward is **regulation** — calm connection, predictable structure, not having demands pile up. For him, a calm day IS the reward.
- Both boys share the same framework — but Kobena's delivery is low-word-high-picture; Nenyi's is verbal + reason-based.
