# SPEECH & LANGUAGE PROGRAMME — NENYI (bespoke)

> **Designed:** 8 Sept 2026 · Delivery: Facilitator (daily reps) + family · aligned to Mission Clinic directive: **monthly advanced SLP review, "to clear him up"**
> **Basis:** Nenyi's profile — unclear speech/mumbling, rushing words; reads aloud nicely (emerging skill); comprehension solid; social over-checking of mum (normal per doctor); no diagnosis.
> **Key principle:** His issue is **articulation + pacing, not language disorder**. He has the words; he needs **clarity + confidence**, not simplified language. Treat him as the bright 10yo he is — the programme should feel like coaching, not therapy.
> **Cadence:** Daily 10–15 min reps + reading-aloud sessions · **Monthly** SLP review (Mission Clinic) to re-assess and progress targets.

---

## BASELINE
- Mumbles; word endings dropped; rushes sentences; volume drops at ends of sentences.
- Reads aloud **nicely when asked** — this is the biggest asset: he can already produce clear speech when the text carries him.
- Over-deliberate socially; checks mum before acting. Low-stakes independence work runs parallel (not part of this programme, but the speech reps double as confidence reps — see below).

## THE CORE MECHANIC: "SAY IT LIKE A STORYTELLER"
His reading-aloud is good *because* the text paces him. The programme systematically transfers that pacing to **spontaneous speech** through graded steps:

**The 4-step ladder (any sentence material):**
1. **Model:** Facilitator/parent says the sentence clearly, slightly exaggerated, at 80% speed.
2. **Unison:** he says it WITH you (no pressure — his voice inside yours).
3. **Echo:** he repeats it alone after you.
4. **Own it:** he says it as HIS answer (question about the story, his opinion, retell).

**Never correct mid-flow.** Let him finish, then model the sentence back once ("I went to the mar**ket**…" with the final word punched) and let him echo. Correction = another model, never "no, say it properly."

## DAILY STRUCTURE (10–15 min, 3 parts)
**A. Articulation warm-up (3 min) — make it silly, not clinical:**
- Tongue twisters, sound games, exaggerated mouth movements in a mirror ("la-li-lu-le-lo", "m-m-m-mango")
- **Volume laddering:** whisper → normal → LOUD on the same phrase (fixes the trailing-off endings)
- **Word-final game:** "finish the word" — you say "baske…", he slams "BALL!" (targets his dropped endings directly)

**B. Reading aloud + comprehension (5–7 min) — the doctor's directive:**
- He reads aloud (his strength) → then **answers questions about it** — not because his comprehension is weak, but because **answering correctly in full sentences is the speech rep**:
  - Level 1 (echo): "Who went to the market?" → he finds + reads the sentence
  - Level 2 (own words): "What did Kofi do?" → **"Kofi went to the market."** (insist on the FULL sentence — this is where clarity is built)
  - Level 3 (predict/opinion): "What do you think happens next? Why?" (builds expressive complexity)
- **The confidence engine:** correct answer → specific praise ("You said that whole sentence clearly — I understood every word") → he wants the next question. That's the loop the doctor prescribed.

**C. Storytelling / retell (2–3 min):** he retells the story in 3 sentences (beginning, middle, end) — first with picture prompts, then from memory. Storytelling is spontaneous-speech practice disguised as fun. Gradually add the **group element**: retell to the family at dinner, to Nana on video call, to Kobena (peer-teaching — also builds the brother bond on his terms).

## SPEECH-FRIENDLY HOME PRACTICES (embedded, no table)
- **1-in-1-out rule:** when he says something mumbled, respond to the *content* but once per conversation model it back clearly: "You **played football** at break?" — he'll confirm clearly. Never interrupt his flow.
- **Wait-time:** count 3 seconds before rescuing him when he stalls — reduces the mum-checking (he checks because he's used to being rescued).
- **Phone-the-relative game:** weekly voice-note to grandad telling what he did today — real audience + natural pacing. (Also gives you dated speech samples.)

## DATA TRACKING (simple, 2 metrics)
1. **Clarity score:** record 30 sec of him reading + 30 sec of free speech **week 1, week 4, week 8** (phone voice note). Compare with him — he hears his own progress (motivation!).
2. **Full-sentence rate:** of his 10 dinner-conversation answers, how many are full clear sentences? (Target: 3/10 → 6/10 by week 8.)

## CADENCE + REVIEW
- **Daily:** parts A+B (Facilitator runs Mon–Fri; family runs weekends)
- **Monthly:** Mission SLP listens to the voice samples + data → adjusts targets ("clear him up" = measurable change month on month)
- **Weekly:** 5-min review with family on Sunday

## WHAT TO AVOID
- Correcting every mumble (kills confidence → increases mum-checking)
- Making him repeat himself more than once before responding (adults "getting" him first-time is the reward for clear speech — use it: "Ask me that clearly and I'll answer straight away")
- Comparing to Kobena's programme; his is about polish, Kobe's is about building from base.
