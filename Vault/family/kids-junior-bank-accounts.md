## Recommendation

1. **Nenyi + Kobena: GCB KidiStar** — H is at branches often (Dome Market, Oyarifa); GCB has 149+ branches, 5% interest, GHS-10-style low entry, free standing orders from H's own GCB account (easy weekly top-up from 2Real sales). Best everyday fit.
2. **If H banks elsewhere / wants stricter saving: CalBank KiddySave** — zero fees and the once-a-year, 20%-cap withdrawal rule is a real discipline lock for long-term savings.
3. Good fallback: **Absa Junior** (GHS 50, no fees, simplest paperwork) if branch queues are a problem.

Practical split suggestion: one liquid account each (KidiStar-style, for gifts/pocket money/term-time top-ups) OR simply KidiStar both + a rule H sets the withdrawal cadence himself. Teens convert at 13/18 — Kobena hits TeenEdge/Teen tier in ~1 year either way.

## Documents to gather for each account (one branch visit)

- H's Ghana Card (original + copy)
- Proof of address (utility bill, or Ghana Post GPS digital address)
- Each child's **birth certificate** (essential for the trust registration)
- 2 passport photos of H (+1 of each child at most banks)
- TIN of H (some banks ask; Ghana Card doubles as TIN now)
- Cash for opening deposit (GHS 50–100 is safe)

One branch visit covers both kids at the same bank — open both accounts same day.

## Kids' factors

- **Kobena (ASD):** skip physical bank visits with him; open accounts for both in one solo trip for H. When Kobena is 13 and gets a teen card, start with ATM withdrawal practice at a single-drill routine. Interest shown on statements is also a natural counting/money lesson if he engages — keep it low-pressure.
- **Nenyi (10):** bring him along if wanted — banks' kids' accounts (KidiStar, Early Savers Club at Access, min GHS 200) include kid-facing literacy activities; good single outing.
- Ask the teller for **SMS alerts to H's phone** on every credit/debit (offered on most trust accounts) so H tracks deposits without statements.
