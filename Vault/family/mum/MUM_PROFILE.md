## 🚶 Daily movement — WALKS (compound or outside the gate)

Walking is now part of Mum's routine, not an occasional outing. She is recorded as **housebound** in the
medical master, so this is deliberately addressing mobility *and* isolation — and it has already paid off before:
**11 Aug she walked at 11:10am and the morning swelling was logged as REDUCED.** (17 Aug: short walk 3:32pm.)

### The routine
- **Short and frequent beats long and rare.** Start with **5–10 minutes** in the compound. Extend only when she
  is comfortable and steady.
- **Twice a day when she's willing** — once in the **cool of the morning**, once **late afternoon / early evening**.
- **Compound first.** Only go **outside the gate** once she is walking the compound confidently — and then
  **always with Stephanie alongside**, not trailing behind.

### Safety rules (non-negotiable — she has a documented fall)
- **Never walk alone.** On **11 Sep she fell** and the post-fall reading was **189/128** (critical). Someone is
  beside her, close enough to hold an arm, for every walk.
- **Check the day's BP first.** Her Furosemide stop-rule is **<100 or >140 systolic**. If the reading is outside
  that, or she's had a high/low day, or she says she feels dizzy or weak — **skip the walk** and tell H.
- **Avoid the midday sun.** Accra heat plus a diuretic is a dehydration risk — her **sodium came back 161
  (high/dehydrated)**. Walk early or late, keep her in shade where possible.
- **Water on hand.** Small sips during and after. Furosemide + heat + walking all pull fluid.
- **Proper footwear** — not slippers. Flat, even ground; watch the gate threshold and any uneven paving.
- **Stop immediately** on dizziness, breathlessness, chest discomfort, or unsteadiness. Sit, rest, recheck BP if needed.
- **Watch the ankles.** Swelling going *down* after a walk is the good sign she showed on 11 Aug; swelling going
  *up*, or new breathlessness, gets reported to H.

### Logging
Every walk gets a line in the daily report, same as the existing entries:
**date · time started · duration · where (compound / outside gate / how far) · who accompanied · how she seemed after
(steady / tired / breathless) · any swelling change.** This is how we build a picture of what she can actually do.

> **Reporting rule:** if she refuses a walk, that is worth noting too — refusal or sudden reluctance to walk can be
> an early sign of pain, dizziness or low mood, and H wants to see it.
