## Job Advert (as published)
**Role:** Facilitator for a Child With Autism — Full-Time
**Overview:** The facilitator provides one-on-one support to a **10-year-old child with autism during school hours**. Primary responsibility: assist the child academically, socially, emotionally and behaviourally to ensure successful participation in classroom and school activities while encouraging independence and confidence.

### Responsibilities
**Classroom / academic support**
- Assist the child in understanding classroom instructions and assignments
- Help the child stay focused and engaged during lessons
- Simplify or repeat instructions when necessary using appropriate communication methods

**Behavioural & emotional support**
- Monitor and manage emotional regulation and sensory needs
- Help the child remain calm and focused during stressful or overstimulating situations
- Encourage healthy interaction with classmates and teachers
- Support the child in developing communication and social skills

**Communication & reporting**
- Maintain regular communication with parents regarding the child's daily progress, challenges, and achievements
- Work collaboratively with teachers, school administrators, therapists, and parents

### Requirements
**Essential skills:** strong patience & emotional intelligence · work calmly under pressure · good communication & interpersonal skills · build trust & rapport with children · strong observation & problem-solving · follow structured routines & behaviour plans · basic classroom support & organisation.

**Minimum qualifications:** SHS certificate or equivalent · experience with children (especially autism/special needs) · clear spoken English · **minimum 1 year experience**.

## 🎤 FACILITATOR INTERVIEW — QUESTION BANK

### Section A — Warm-up / motivation (2 min)
1. Why do you want to support a child with autism?
2. What does "being a guide, not just a supervisor" mean to you?

### Section B — Experience & autism understanding (core)
3. Walk me through your experience with children — how much was with autism or special needs?
4. This child communicates **non-verbally and via signs/visuals**. How would you figure out what he needs if he can't tell you in words?
5. What do you know about autism — what does good support look like in a classroom?

### Section C — Behavioural scenarios (the screening heart)
6. **Overstimulation:** He becomes overwhelmed in a noisy classroom and starts to close up/fidget. Walk me through exactly what you'd do, minute by minute.
7. **Transition:** He is happily playing but must move to the next lesson. How do you help him left calmly? (Watch for: warning, "now/next", "finished box", sign-based cues.)
8. **Interest led:** He only wants to repeat one activity (e.g. push cars). How do you challenge this into a learning moment without frustration?
9. **Understanding:** He doesn't respond to a spoken instruction. What do you do?
10. **Sensory overload / meltdown in a stressful moment:** Tell me how you would keep him calm and safe — and keep yourself calm.

### Section D — Communication & team
11. How will you report the child's **daily progress, challenges and wins** to his parents?
12. How do you stay consistent with **teachers, therapists and the birth plan** when everyone has different styles?
13. Are you willing to learn **Makaton Level 1** and the family's visual systems and routines?

### Section E — Natural approach & values alignment
14. What's your view on a **natural/gut-focused approach** to support (organic food, reduced sugar/grains, detox under clinician care)?
15. How consistent are you with disciplined routines? Give an example of a routine you followed precisely.

### Section F — Practical close
16. What questions do you have for us?
17. Confirm **availability** (school hours), **transport**, **start date**, and **salary expectation**.
18. Two references — who are they?

### Scoring notes (Interviewer)
- **Red flags:** no concrete scenario answers, dismissive of visuals/signs, unwilling to train, blames the child, inflated self-scores, can't describe staying calm.
- **Green flags:** uses the child's own interest, mentions consistency/visuals/advanced-warning unprompted, calm tone, concrete examples, asks about the child's specific needs.
