## TIER 1 — FOUNDATIONAL (read-first, most signal for where you are now)

### The E-Myth Revisited — Michael Gerber
*Why for H:* You're a hands-on owner who burns weeds at 4am. This book nails the trap: working *in* your business (as the technician) vs working *on* it (as the entrepreneur). The Dome Market walk-in, the Oyarifa warehouse, the farm — each is a separate "business" that needs systems, not just sweat.
*Key concept:* The franchise prototype. Build the business so it runs without you for a day. You're not there yet — farm build needs you, retail needs you — but the direction is clear.
*How I reference it:* When you tell me about another 4am site visit, I'll ask "was that CEO-H or technician-H?"

### Never Eat Alone — Keith Ferrazzi
*Why for H:* The single best networking book for a real operator. Ferrazzi's core: networking is about generosity before transactions. Ghana's relationship-first culture makes this *more* applicable here than in the US market it was written for. The Ghana Spurs meetup is a textbook Never Eat Alone move — join a community first, do business later.
*Key concept:* The "weak tie" — your next deal won't come from your close friends, it'll come from the friend-of-a-friend you met once.
*How I reference it:* When I push you toward the Spurs group, the entrepreneur club, the Rotary equivalent — that's Never Eat Alone in practice.

### The Lean Startup — Eric Ries
*Why for H:* You run multiple ventures on thin margins. Ries's "build-measure-learn" loop is literally what you need for the farm — don't build the whole apiary, start with 3 hives, measure, learn, scale. Same for new product lines at Dome.
*Key concept:* Validated learning. Test the smallest version before betting big.

### Million Dollar Weekend — Noah Kagan
*Why for H:* Short, actionable, no-fluff. Kagan's "ask 100 people in 48 hours" method is perfect for the FB and WhatsApp groups — test products by asking, not guessing.
*Key concept:* The fear cure — most business paralysis is solved by one uncomfortable conversation.

### Smart Broke Dumb Rich — Zor Veyl
*Why for H:* A 124-page hammer. Veyl's argument — being "smart" (overthinking, caution, analysis) keeps you poor. Taking imperfect action beats perfect planning. You already live this (4am site visits, hands-on deals) — this book validates the instinct and sharpens it.
*Key concept:* "Stop Thinking. Start Earning." — motion beats perfection, every time.
*How I reference it:* When you're hesitating on a decision, I'll say "Smart Broke Dumb Rich — what would action look like right now?"

### The Richest Man in Babylon — George Clason
*Why for H:* A short classic that every entrepreneur should read once. Seven parables about money — save 10% of everything you earn, make your money work, get good advice before investing. Directly applicable to 2Real's cash flow and the farm build.
*Key lesson:* "Pay yourself first" — every cedi that comes through 2Real, 10% is yours before any expense.
*How I reference it:* When we talk about separating business cash from personal wealth.

### Scivive — Richard Heart
*Why for H:* Heart's book on longevity and self-optimization. The premise: your body and mind are your first business assets. If you're burned out, sick, or running on bad sleep, every business decision suffers. For a 52-year-old running three ventures, this is maintenance you can't skip.
*Key concept:* Health is not separate from wealth — it's the foundation of it.
*Note from Coach:* Read this after the first 5 books. It's not urgent, but don't skip it forever.

## TIER 2 — NETWORKING & RELATIONSHIPS (your §6 lane)

### How to Win Friends and Influence People — Dale Carnegie
*Why for H:* Old, famous, and still the best. The chapter on "be genuinely interested in the other person" is the Ghana networking rule boiled down. At the Spurs meetup: ask about *them* before mentioning 2Real.
*Key lesson:* A name is everything. Write down names. Use them. Carnegie's "the sweetest sound to any person is their own name" — in Ghana, this separates a foreigner from someone who belongs.

### Give and Take — Adam Grant
*Why for H:* Grant proves that the most successful networkers are *givers*, not takers or matchers. But the key — givers who set boundaries succeed; givers who burn out fail. For H: give introductions, share knowledge, be generous — but protect your margins.
*Key concept:* The giver's dilemma — give without being exploited.

### The 20-Minute Networking Meeting — Marcia Ballinger
*Why for H:* You're time-poor. This book shows how to do a networking coffee in 20 minutes that actually produces a follow-up. Structured, repeatable, no wasted time.

### Superconnector — Scott Gerber & Ryan Paugh
*Why for H:* For when you're ready to become the person who connects *other* people. That's the level where networking becomes a moat: when you're the hub, deals come to you.

## TIER 3 — RETAIL, HARDWARE & TRADING (your core business)

### Delivering Happiness — Tony Hsieh
*Why for H:* Zappos built a billion-dollar business on customer service. For Dome Market: every interaction matters. Hsieh's point — the only marketing that works long-term is making customers who tell others about you.

### Why We Buy — Paco Underhill
*Why for H:* Retail anthropology. How people walk through a store, what they look at, how they decide. For Dome Market layout and product placement.

### The Challenger Sale — Matthew Dixon
*Why for H:* For B2B/wholesale (tools to contractors, hardware to builders). The book shows that the best salespeople teach, not please. H knows tools — teach the buyer why UK-imported matters.

### Small Business, Big Money — Akin Alabi
*Why for H:* Nigerian author, written for West African entrepreneurs. Practical, no-theory, street-level. Covers inventory management, pricing for the local market, and why "buy cheap, sell high" fails.

### How to Sell to Nigerians — Akin Alabi
*Why for H:* Directly relevant — Ghana's market psychology overlaps heavily with Nigeria's. Covers trust-building, payment culture, and the "show me" buyer mentality.

## TIER 4 — FARMING & AGRIBUSINESS (Senya Beraku)

### The One-Straw Revolution — Masanobu Fukuoka
*Why for H:* Not a business book — a philosophy book about farming with nature instead of against it. For Senya Beraku: minimum intervention, maximum observation. The apiary fits this perfectly — bees don't need managing, they need a healthy environment.

### The Market Gardener — Jean-Martin Fortier
*Why for H:* Small-scale, high-yield farming on limited land. His methods work on 1-2 acres and produce commercial volumes. Perfect for the Senya Beraku transition from build-phase to production-phase.

### Beekeeping for Beginners — various (find the one for West Africa)
*Why for H:* The apiary is your lowest-input, highest-margin farm venture. Proper hive management is the difference between honey as a side product and honey as a consistent revenue stream.

### Starting a Farm in Africa — (search for Ghana-specific)
*Why for H:* Infrastructure, labour, weather patterns specific to the region.

## TIER 5 — MINDSET & DISCIPLINE (your personal growth)

### Atomic Habits — James Clear
*Why for H:* You already have the discipline (4am site visits). Clear shows how to make those habits *automatic* so they cost zero willpower. The farm check, the 12:30 Status post, the Sunday group post — habit-stack them.
*Key concept:* Habit stacking — pair a new habit with an existing one.

### The Hard Thing About Hard Things — Ben Horowitz
*Why for H:* The best "I've been there" book for entrepreneurs who are going through it. Horowitz talks about laying people off, making impossible choices, and the loneliness of being the boss. For H's real moments.

### Meditations — Marcus Aurelius
*Why for H:* The original stoic. For the mornings at 4am when the business feels like it's all on you. The Stoics' answer: focus only on what you can control.
