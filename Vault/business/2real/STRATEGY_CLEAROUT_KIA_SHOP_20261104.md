# Strategy — Clear-out, sell-while-free, shop-to-rent, Kia Rio (brainstormed 04/11/26)

> Goal: **cash out tired/dead stock fast → free up space → get hands-free so you're selling without being tied to the shop → rent the shop → sell the Kia Rio and buy a better car.**

## PART A — The Dome clear-out (dead/tired goods → wholesalers)

### The mechanism you're already running (keep it)
Yellow/orange **Amazon bags** = bulk lots to wholesalers. Works because wholesalers buy *condition-blind* at a discount and re-sort the resellable bits themselves. This is the **one-off-goods exit** your core shift wants ("liquidate old stock").

### Bag-lot pricing logic (don't underprice, don't itemize)
- **Retail value of bag contents × 0.25–0.35** = quick wholesale target. You clear + yield, they take the sorting labour.
- Price **per bag at a fixed number** ("yellow bag, 40 items, works-or-not, GHS 250") — buyers stop haggling per-item if you refuse to open the bag.
- Group by **story**: "repair & parts lot" (non-working fans/microwaves/DVDs — repairers love this), "yoga & fitness lot", "IT lot". Buyers who want one category pay more per bag.

### SPACE first — because space is the product
Every bag out = floor you can rent. Track bag-out against **space freed (m² or % of floor)**, not just cash. You're not clearing goods, you're **converting dead stock into empty lettable floor** + proceeds.

## PART D — Kia Rio (sell → buy better car)

### Why selling is the sound call (your reasons, validated)
1. **RHD→LHD conversion ran but "not as perfect as expected."** Imperfect conversion = valuation ceiling + future repair liability on you. Better to exit to a buyer who wants the project than hold a half-done conversion that depreciates as a daily driver.
2. **Capital release** — car is **illiquid + depreciating + demanding insurance/maintenance**. Cash out → buy clean, drive it, or redeploy.
3. **Buying another car** with proceeds = you define the spec, from a clean baseline, no retrofit risk.
