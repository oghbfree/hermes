# Future-proof from gov.uk laws / bottlenecks

This summary focuses on the actual friction points the user will hit on the Ghana trip and in running 2 Real Enterprises from UK supply chains, plus any regulatory landmines that can quietly stop a small business. It’s not a generic trade essay — it’s stop/go guidance.

---

## 1. Leaving UK / personal goods
**Gov UK law that bites:** cash declaration + goods for personal use.

Actions
- Bring less than £10,000 cash into Ghana without declaring it at border control. Go over and file form at HMRC/UK border + Ghana customs.
- Used electronics, tools, or new in-box items that look commercial: declare them on arrival or Ghana customs can seize and treat as commercial imports with higher duty.
- Keep receipts/proof of purchase for all items carried in luggage (CCTV parts, tools, phones). If Ghana asks “for resale?”, receipts answer the question.

---

## 2. UK export controls on dual-use electronics
**Real risk:** Some network/routing gear and imaging hardware can fall under UK Export Control Order 2008 dual-use rules (radar, RF, image processing). Consumer CPE routers and CCTV gear are typically OK, but:

Actions
- For earler high-gain antenna kit or industrial-grade routers, check the UK SPIRE system if the seller advertises military/industrial capability. For the LB-Link / MTN ZLT route, this is low risk.
- If dealing with a seller who asks about end-use or destination country, answer truthfully — false statements invalidate export licence exceptions.

---

## 3. UK customs / VAT on items shipped to Ghana
**Bottleneck:** Even when you import from the UK into Ghana, UK exit VAT is not claimable by non-UK residents on personal goods after 1 January 2021 for most categories. Post-Brexit, VAT and customs are destination-country problems.

Actions
- Expect supplier to charge UK VAT if they sell via domestic UK fulfillment and don’t export. Ask supplier for export invoice with zero-rated VAT + proof of export (bill of lading / tracking to Ghana).
- Courier companies may pre-pay Ghana customs and bill you later — this is normal and creates a cash-flow delay.

---

## 4. Ghana import requirements for electronics / CCTV / routers
**Real friction:** Ghana Revenue Authority (GRA) / customs require:
- Shipper’s export declaration + proforma invoice + bill of lading/airway bill. Packing list helps.
- HS code for each item. For routers/cameras/cabling, typical codes are 8517 / 8525 / 8544 — declare correctly to avoid re-classification.
- Some used electronics can attract “used goods” scrutiny and opens an inspection lane that adds 3–14 days.

Actions
- Friend’s invoice should list: description, quantity, unit value, total value, incoterm, HS code, country of origin. The more explicit, the faster customs.
- Avoid shipping lithium batteries loose; pack them inside the devices they belong to.

---

## 5. Mobile network / spectrum constraints
**What’s actually enforced:** MTN and AirtelTigo own the spectrum; SIM registration is mandatory by Ghana NCA rules. The CPE router solutions you picked are standard residential/consumer kit and widely accepted, but:

Actions
- Buy the SIM in Ghana using Ghana Card or NIA verification. Bring a fully-registered SIM if possible; seamless activation reduces trip friction.
- Avoid modified firmware or non-brand antennas that claim “unlocked bands” — resale-grade modifications can breach equipment type approval if inspected.

---

## 6. Business operations / 2 Real Enterprises risk
**The real bottlenecks:**
- UK sourcing and import to Ghana without a Ghanaian import/export licence for certain controlled goods is legal for commercial inventory, but the licence application takes time. For now small-batch commercial resale is usually handled by a shipping agent or customs broker.
- Tax compliance: 2 Real Enterprises should have TIN and be VAT-registered once turnover exceeds thresholds. Non-compliance triggers penalties from GRA.
- Consumer goods descriptions on invoices must match actual condition. Mis-describing “used but like-new” phones as “new” can be treated as fraud under Ghana Consumer Protection law.

Actions
- Pick one customs broker and create a written SOP for them: what docs you send, HS codes, who pays duty (DAP vs DDP). Jakmob crew shouldn’t be guessing at the border.
- Use zobaze receipts as your pricing evidence — if GRA audits your stock values, these back up your ₵493K inventory.

---

## 7. UK law that could trip you up later
**Non-negotiable rules:**
- Money Laundering Regulations 2017: if any customer pays cash over €1,000 (≈ ₵ equivalent), you must do customer due diligence (CDD). Keep simple KYC register even if informal for now.
- Online sales to UK/EU customers: Consumer Rights Act 2015 + returns under 14 days applies if you ever sell online into the UK/EU digital or physical store. Not likely for Akoma/2Real this trip, but for any future web sales — a 2-paragraph returns policy protects you.
- GDPR / data protection: CCTV, phone logs, CRM data of customers needs a lawful basis and retention limits. The cheapest risk-reduction: delete customer messages after 12–24 months unless actively relevant.

## 8. Practical stop/go checklist

GO:
- Buy LB-Link or MTN ZLT router in UK/Ghana — consumer model, keep receipt and invoice consistent with declared value.
- Carry receipts for all physical stock/electronics.
- Request export invoice with zero UK VAT from supplier.
- Ship batteries packed inside devices.

SLOW DOWN / CHECK:
- High-value bulk electronics (> £6,000 declared value) — may trigger UK export notification and Ghana customs inspection. Break into smaller shipments.
- “Used” declared condition — be accurate; fraud line is thin.
- Cash > £10,000 — declare.

STOP / GET HELP:
- Controlled goods (radar, encryption devices, industrial RF). Ask seller for end-user statement and SPIRE check.
- Customs seizure — stop here, don’t negotiate on the spot; contact customs broker and get a written justification.
