🔔 Dad's 3-Day Check — 31 Aug 2026

**Health Intelligence:**
• DVT prevention matters for wheelchair-bound elders — a 2026 study of long-term bedridden patients found intermittent pneumatic compression (IPC) devices plus daily care cut blood-clot risk sharply (13% vs 33%). Regular leg/foot movement also helps circulation.
• After a below-knee amputation, daily checks of the residual limb (skin hygiene, pressure points, swelling) remain the key lifelong habit for protecting tissue and avoiding wounds.
• Phantom limb sensations are common and normal — they tend to ease over time.

**Wellbeing Tips:**
• Reposition in the chair every couple of hours, and avoid keeping the knee bent all day — a constantly flexed knee can tighten (contracture) and make sitting and transfers harder.
• Keep fluids up: aim for 6–8 cups daily. Older wheelchair-bound adults are prone to dehydration, which worsens confusion, blood-pressure swings and constipation.
• Protein-rich, nutrient-dense meals help preserve strength and muscle.
• Regular social contact is a proven buffer against low mood after amputation — a call, visit or group keeps spirits up. Good sleep also supports mood.

**Action Items (if any):**
• Ask the care team whether compression stockings or IPC would be appropriate for Dad given his dilated blood vessel.
• Check residual limb skin at each care visit.
• Watch for leg swelling, redness or warmth (DVT warning signs).
