# Case Study: "House 33" — Contemporary White Villa (rendered project set)

**Source:** 20 architectural renderings of ONE luxury contemporary villa, documented from every angle.
**Identifying marks:** gate number "33"; leaf-pattern cut-out metal security screens; gatehouse/guard booth; wood-slatted carport with a red sports car + white luxury sedan; white perimeter walls; flat roofs; rooftop terraces; stacked-stone feature walls.
**Why it matters:** This is the most *complete* single-project visual set in the library — a full exterior + landscape + detail study of one coherent design language. Treat it as a "finished product" reference: how all the pieces (from the earlier Oasis + G013 references) come together into a built-looking home.

**Images (20):** see `INDEX.md`. Grouped below by what they teach.

---

## 1. The Design Language (consistent across all 20)

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Base palette | Stark white stucco/render walls, flat roofs w/ parapet |
| Warm accent | Horizontal/vertical **wood slats** (pergolas, carport ceilings, privacy screens, rooflines) |
| Texture accent | **Stacked/ribbed stone** feature columns & entry walls (light grey) |
| Glazing | Floor-to-ceiling black-framed glass, glass railings on balconies/terraces |
| Structure | Thick grey concrete pillars supporting flat overhangs/porte-cochère |
| Hardscape | Large light-grey stone pavers / polished concrete driveways & decks |
| Softscape | Manicured green lawn, spherical bushes, palm trees, cypress, autumn trees, flower beds (red/pink/purple), ornamental grasses |

**Education point:** A *limited material palette* (white + wood + stone + black glass + grey) repeated with discipline is what makes these read as "luxury" rather than busy. Every image uses the same 5 materials. That restraint is the lesson.

---

## 2. Spatial Organization (from aerials)

- **Clustered / pavilion layout** (not one box): main 2–3 story block + separate single-story wings (guest cabana, patio pavilions) + pool + carport + gatehouse.
- **Main residence:** 2–3 stories. Ground = open-plan living w/ sliding glass to patio; upper = bedrooms w/ balconies + glass railings; rooftop = terrace w/ pergola (the "sky lounge" idea, built).
- **Carport:** open-sided, wood-plank ceiling, 2 luxury cars, recessed LEDs — *not* enclosed garage (note: differs from G013's 2-car enclosed garage; carport = cheaper, more "showcase").
- **Pool:** long rectangular, light-blue, grey coping, placed between cabana and main house. Sometimes a separate small **plunge pool** by the terrace.
- **Gatehouse / guard booth:** small white glass structure by the gate — security typology.
- **Perimeter:** tall OR low white walls + black leaf-pattern metal screens.

**Cross-ref:** This clusters like the Oasis-243 concept but *executed* in the white-wood-stone language of a modern Accra villa — and the vertical zoning matches the G013 lesson (ground=living, up=sleeping, top=terrace).

## 4. Lighting Strategy (recurring, worth copying)

- **Recessed/soffit LEDs** under every roof overhang & pergola (warm white)
- **Wall sconces** on stone feature walls (up/down wash)
- **Landscape up-lights** on trees & bushes
- **Linear driveway edge lights**
- **Underwater pool + spa LEDs**
- **Glass-railing + interior glow** at dusk

**Education point:** Lighting is *layered* (architectural + landscape + water), all warm-toned, all hidden sources. That's why the twilight/night shots look expensive.

---

## 5. Accra-Relevance Watch-outs

- **White stucco + flat roof** = the same waterproofing risk flagged in Oasis-243 & G013. Parapet + proper fall essential in our rain.
- **Carport (open) vs garage (enclosed):** carport showcases cars but exposes them to sun/dust — in Accra heat, enclosed or at least shaded parking may suit better. Trade-off to note.
- **Gatehouse/guard booth** = real local security norm for high-end builds; budget it.
- **Leaf-pattern metal screens** = decorative + security without razor wire (same humane-security theme as Oasis-243's barb-less edge).
- **Tall white walls** = privacy, but confirm permitted height & boundary-setback bylaws.
- **Wood slats outdoors** = need weather-resistant species (teak/ipê) or they'll grey/rot fast in our humidity. Spec the wood.
