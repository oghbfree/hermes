## ⚠️ Critical Risks

### 1. Property 19 is underwater in EVERY scenario
Even at best case £350k valuation → investor pays £245k. Your mortgage is £268k. **Property 19 alone loses money even before the 30% haircut.** Property 21's equity is subsidising property 19 throughout.

### 2. The IOU is the real deal — not the cash
- Best case: you walk away with ~£103k cash, but £85k is deferred
- Worst case: you walk away with ~£23k cash, but £165k is deferred
- **60-75% of your return depends on the investor paying in year one**

### 3. Service charge arrears
- Combined: £13,400 in arrears
- These typically need to be cleared on or before completion
- The buyer/investor may insist these are settled from sale proceeds
- **This reduces your net proceeds further** — factor it in

### 4. Enforcing the charge on personal property
- Sounds protective but enforcing it = solicitors = time = cost = stress
- If the investor's personal property is already mortgaged, your charge sits behind the mortgage lender
- Need to verify the investor actually owns property outright (or has sufficient equity)

## 🏠 Speaking to Tenant in 21A — Strategy

This is smart. Options to present:

**Option A: "Pay advance rent, I'll keep the property"**
- Ask the tenant: can you pay 6-12 months in advance?
- If yes, you keep property 21 as a rental asset
- You only sell property 19 (which is the problem child anyway)
- Pro: keeps a performing asset, avoids the messy deal
- Con: tenant may not have that cash

**Option B: "I'm selling, you'll need to work with the new owner"**
- Inform them a sale is happening
- The investor/ buyer inherits the tenancy
- Pro: clean exit
- Con: you lose property 21's equity

**Option C: "Vacant possession increases the sale price"**
- If the tenant can move out before completion
- Vacant possession = higher valuation = better deal
- Offer them a relocation incentive (say £1-2k)
- Pro: maximises property 21's value
- Con: time pressure, tenant may refuse

**Recommendation**: Start with Option A. If the tenant can pay advance rent, you keep property 21 (which is the profitable one) and sell property 19 separately. Property 19 alone at £275k might even clear with less drama.

## ⚖️ Legal Charge / Lien on Property

Yes, this is standard in deferred payment deals:

- A **legal charge** is registered at the Land Registry against the investor's property
- It gives you security: if they don't pay, you can force a sale of that property (in theory)
- **But** you need:
  - **Your own solicitor** to draft and register the charge — NOT the investor's solicitor
  - **Verification** that the investor's property is unencumbered or has sufficient equity
  - **Clear terms**: interest rate, payment schedule, default triggers, enforcement timeline
  - **Personal guarantee** from the investor (not just the property)

**Critical**: If the investor's property already has a mortgage, your charge sits behind the lender. If they default and you try to enforce, the mortgage lender gets paid first. You need to know the loan-to-value on their property.
