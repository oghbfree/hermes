# DRIVER CODE OF CONDUCT
## Transporting Mrs. Comfort Blankson — Safety Protocols

---

**Effective Date:** June 2026
**Applies to:** All drivers transporting Comfort Blankson (including John, George, hired drivers, or any designated person)

---

## 1. VEHICLE REQUIREMENTS

Before any trip with Comfort, the driver MUST confirm:

- [ ] **Working Air Conditioning** — must be on and cooling before Mum enters the vehicle. Heat exhaustion during the drive can cause a health crisis.
- [ ] **Clean vehicle interior** — no clutter on seats or floor
- [ ] **Memory foam / anti-vibration cushion** on Mum's seat (to minimise pothole impact on spine/hips)
- [ ] **Seatbelt functional** on Mum's seat
- [ ] **Window shades or tinted windows** — to block direct sun on Mum
- [ ] **Cold bottled water** in the vehicle (minimum 2 bottles)
- [ ] **Emergency Red-Bag** in the vehicle (meds, BP monitor, fan, medical history)
- [ ] **Phone charger** available for nurse's phone

## 2. DRIVING STANDARDS

### Speed Limits
- **Urban areas (Weija, Accra):** Maximum 40 km/h
- **Highways:** Maximum 80 km/h
- **School zones:** 30 km/h — always
- **NEVER exceed the speed limit** — Mr Blankson will not tolerate reckless driving with his mother

### Pothole Avoidance
- Ghana roads have significant potholes. The driver must:
  - **Scan the road ahead** constantly
  - **Slow down** for every pothole — go around if possible, never hit at speed
  - **Avoid road edges** where potholes are worst
  - **Use the smoothest lane** — even if it means driving slower
  - The nurse should sit in the back with Mum and monitor her comfort

### Smooth Driving
- **No sudden braking** — brake gently and early
- **No sudden acceleration** — pull away slowly
- **No sharp turns** — take corners wide and slow
- **Maintain steady speed** — avoid stop-start driving where possible
- **Indicate early** — give other drivers plenty of warning

### AC Settings
- **Temperature:** 22–24°C (comfortable, not cold)
- **Airflow:** Directed away from Mum's face — towards ceiling or floor
- **Recirculate mode:** Use in heavy traffic to keep cool air in
- **Check every 30 minutes** that AC is still working

## 3. PICKUP & DROP-OFF PROCEDURES

### Picking Up Mum:
1. Arrive 10 minutes early
2. Start the car and turn on AC **before** Mum comes out
3. Have cold water ready
4. Open the door for Mum — assist her in (do not let her climb in alone)
5. Help with seatbelt
6. Place cushion if not already in position
7. Confirm she is comfortable before driving
8. Nurse sits in back with Mum (or front passenger if nurse is driving)

### Dropping Off Mum:
1. Park as close to the entrance as possible
2. Turn off engine, exit first
3. Open Mum's door — assist her out slowly
4. Ensure she is steady on her feet before letting go
5. Walk her to the door
6. Confirm she is inside and comfortable before leaving

---

## 4. DURING THE TRIP

### The Nurse's Role (in the car):
- Monitor Mum's comfort throughout
- Ensure she drinks water (500ml per 2 hours of travel)
- Check for signs of dizziness, nausea, or distress
- Communicate any concerns to the driver immediately
- For trips over 1 hour: stop for a 10-minute break (the "Kasoa Stretch" principle)

### The Driver's Role:
- Focus on the road — no phone calls while driving
- No loud music — keep volume low
- No smoking in the vehicle — ever
- No alcohol — zero tolerance, even from the night before
- If Mum or the nurse says "stop" — pull over safely and immediately

---

## 5. TRAVEL RULES

- **NO Trotro Policy:** Under no circumstances is Mum to travel by public transport (Trotro, bus, shared taxi). Only private vehicle with a designated driver.
- **No "dropping and picking up"** — the driver stays with the vehicle at all times during stops
- **No unauthorised passengers** — only Mum, the nurse, and approved persons in the vehicle
- **No detours** — follow the agreed route unless directed otherwise by Mr Blankson or the nurse

---

## 6. EMERGENCY PROCEDURES WHILE DRIVING

If Mum becomes unwell during the trip:
1. Pull over safely — do not stop in traffic
2. Call the nurse to assess Mum
3. Call Mr Blankson immediately
4. If serious — call 112 (Ghana emergency)
5. If near a hospital — drive directly there
6. Do NOT continue the journey until Mum is stable
