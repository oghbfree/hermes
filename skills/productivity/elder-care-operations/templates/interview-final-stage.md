## INTERVIEW STRUCTURE

### PART 1: WELCOME & CONTEXT (5 mins)

**Script:**
*"Thank you for coming back. You're one of two candidates left — that itself tells you something. This interview is more detailed. I want to understand how you'd actually do the day-to-day, and I want to be transparent about what I expect. It's a big role — my mum is 91, she has diabetes, high blood pressure, arthritis, and some swelling in her legs. She needs someone who's here not just physically but mentally. Let's get into it."*

**Observe:**
- Body language — confident or nervous?
- Eye contact
- Phone etiquette (is phone out? on silent?)
- First impression warmth

---

### PART 2: COOKING & NUTRITION (10 mins)

**Questions:**

1. *"Walk me through how you'd prepare breakfast for my mum. She's diabetic and has high blood pressure — what does that mean for what you'd cook?"*

2. *"Show me — if you were in the kitchen right now, what would you make for lunch? Describe it step by step."*

3. *"My mum sometimes has trouble with hard foods — how do you adapt meals?"*

4. *"How do you go to market? Do you go yourself? How do you decide what to buy?"*

5. *"Can you plan a full day's meals — breakfast, lunch, dinner — that are diabetic-friendly and low salt?"*

**Scoring:**

| Meal planning ability | | |
| Market/shopping competence | | |

**Red flags:** Can't describe a meal from scratch, suggests ready meals, no understanding of diabetic diet

### PART 3: HEALTH MONITORING & CLINICAL SKILLS (10 mins)

**Questions:**

1. *"How do you take blood pressure? Walk me through it — from picking up the machine to recording the number."*

2. *"My mum's BP is normally around 120/70. Today it's 160/95. What do you do?"*

3. *"How do you check blood sugar? When should it be checked?"*

4. *"My mum's blood sugar is 3.2. She's conscious but shaky. What do you do?"*

5. *"What's a normal pulse rate for an elderly person? What would worry you?"*

6. *"How do you check oxygen saturation? What number is dangerous?"*

7. *"My mum falls in the bathroom. She's on the floor, conscious, says her hip hurts. What do you do?"*

**Scoring:**
| Criteria | Score (1-5) | Notes |
|----------|-------------|-------|
| BP measurement knowledge | | |
| Glucose monitoring knowledge | | |
| Emergency response (hypoglycaemia) | | |
| Emergency response (fall) | | |
| Oxygen saturation knowledge | | |
| Overall clinical awareness | | |

**Red flags:** Can't describe basic vitals procedure, doesn't know hypoglycaemia response, would move a fallen person without assessing injury first

### PART 4: DAILY ROUTINE & TIME MANAGEMENT (10 mins)

## POST-INTERVIEW DECISION FRAMEWORK

### Immediate (same day):
1. Complete scoring sheet while fresh
2. Write 3 bullet points: what impressed, what concerned, gut feeling
3. Compare against previous interview notes

### Within 24 hours:
1. Call references (at least 1 per candidate)
2. Discuss with Ebony — her input matters
3. Check NMC registration (if applicable) — verify at nmc.gov.gh

### Decision criteria:
- **Must-haves (disqualifying if absent):**
  - Genuine warmth and care motivation
  - Basic clinical knowledge (BP, glucose, emergency response)
  - Accepts CCTV
  - Accepts "my way" training approach
  - Available to start within 2 weeks

- **Nice-to-haves (tiebreaker):**
  - Own equipment
  - Electric bike capability
  - Previous elderly care experience
  - NMC registered

### Offer:
- Top candidate gets verbal offer within 48 hours
- Written contract within 1 week
- Start date: within 2 weeks
- 30-day and 60-day review dates set

### Backup:
- If top candidate declines or fails reference check → offer to #2
- Keep #2 warm — don't reject until #1 has signed
