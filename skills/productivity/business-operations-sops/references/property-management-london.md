## Rent Collection

### Process
1. Rent due date: [Date per tenancy agreement]
2. Payment method: Bank transfer (preferred) or standing order
3. If rent not received by due date + 3 days: send polite reminder
4. If rent not received by due date + 7 days: formal notice (H decides next steps)
5. All rent received goes to H's designated account

### Late Payment Protocol
- Day 1-3: No action (grace period)
- Day 4-7: Polite WhatsApp/text reminder
- Day 8-14: Formal written notice (email + letter)
- Day 15+: H decides — escalate to agent or legal

## Maintenance & Repairs

### Routine Maintenance
- Boiler service: Annual (before winter)
- Gas safety certificate: Annual (legal requirement)
- Electrical inspection: Every 5 years (EICR)
- Smoke/CO detectors: Test monthly, replace batteries annually
- Gutter cleaning: Twice yearly (spring and autumn)

### Emergency Repairs
- **Definition:** Issue that poses safety risk or causes significant damage if not addressed immediately (burst pipe, gas leak, electrical fault, no heating in winter)
- **Action:** Contact emergency contractor immediately → notify H with cost estimate
- **Budget limit:** H sets emergency spend authority (e.g., up to £500 without prior approval)

### Non-Emergency Repairs
- Tenant reports issue → log in property management system
- H approves contractor and cost
- Schedule repair within 14 days (or sooner if tenant impact is high)
- Keep all receipts and invoices

## Tenant Management

### New Tenant Onboarding
1. Right to Rent check (legal requirement)
2. Tenancy agreement signed (AST — Assured Shorthold Tenancy)
3. Inventory and schedule of condition (photographs + written)
4. Deposit lodged with DPS (Deposit Protection Service) within 30 days
5. Collect: 1 month rent in advance + deposit (usually 5 weeks)
6. Provide: How to Rent guide, gas safety cert, EPC, EICR
7. Hand over keys

### During Tenancy
- Respect tenant's right to quiet enjoyment (24 hours notice for visits)
- Respond to repair requests within 48 hours
- Annual property inspection (with 24 hours written notice)
- Keep all communication professional and documented

### End of Tenancy
1. Serve Section 21 notice (minimum 2 months) or mutual agreement
2. Final inspection: compare to inventory, photograph everything
3. Deposit return: agree deductions with tenant → DPS processes
4. If deductions disputed: DPS adjudication
5. Change locks between tenants

## Insurance
- Buildings insurance: Maintained on both properties
- Landlord insurance: Consider (covers rent loss, liability, legal)
- Review annually before renewal

## Tax & Compliance
- Declare rental income on Self Assessment tax return
- Mortgage interest: tax relief rules (check current year)
- Allowable expenses: repairs, insurance, agent fees, legal fees
- Keep all records for minimum 6 years
- EPC rating: minimum E (legal requirement to let)
- HMO license: if applicable (5+ unrelated tenants)

## Property Sale Process (When H Decides)
1. Get RICS valuation (2-3 agents for comparison)
2. Choose agent or sell privately
3. Prepare: EPC, fixtures and fittings form, property information form
4. Conveyancing solicitor instructed
5. Accept offer → exchange contracts → complete
6. Capital Gains Tax: calculate and budget for (if applicable)
7. **£120,000 expected from property sale** — H to allocate: debt, safety net, business investment

## Pitfalls
- **Never skip Right to Rent checks** — £3,000 fine per tenant
- **Always protect deposit in DPS** — 3x deposit penalty if you don't
- **Keep gas safety current** — criminal offence if not done
- **Don't self-manage without understanding landlord law** — consider agent if time is limited
- **Document everything** — tenant disputes go to whoever has the paperwork
- **Don't spend property sale proceeds without a plan** — H's allocation strategy first
